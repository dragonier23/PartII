## Assignment 2: Applying Word2Vec to Downstream Tasks

### Q1. What are the main tasks in Assignment 2?

There are two main tasks:

1. **Unsupervised PoS tagging using K-Means**
2. **Unsupervised extractive summarisation**

You should complete both the implementation and the required discussion.

### Q2. Do I need to keep the outputs in my notebook?

Yes.

Please keep all relevant experimental outputs and make sure that the notebook is fully executed before submission.

### Q3. Will our code be rerun?

A sample of submissions may be randomly selected and executed.

Your submitted code should therefore run correctly, and the reproduced results should be within a reasonable reproducible range of the results recorded in your notebook.

### Q4. What kind of analysis is expected for the PoS-tagging task?

You are free to choose an interesting question to investigate.

Possible directions include, but are not limited to:

* whether the evaluation metrics are appropriate;
* strengths and weaknesses of the metrics.
* 
You do not need to cover all of these. It is usually better to investigate one or two questions carefully than to mention many questions superficially. Note, we expect you to investigate something new (other than the listed ones) on your own.

### Q5. What kind of analysis is expected for the summarisation task?

You may investigate similar questions concerning evaluation metrics, hyperparameters and training domains.

Similarily, we expect you to ask something interesting and explore it on your own, other than the ones that we listed.

### Q6. Are negative results acceptable?

Yes.

Both positive and negative results are welcome.

The important part is that you formulate an interesting question, investigate it using appropriate experiments, and try to explain **why** you obtained the observed result.

### Q7. Is simply reporting the numerical results enough?

The discussion should go beyond describing which number is larger.

We expect you to interpret the results and provide reasoning or evidence that helps explain them.

### Q8. Should the discussion include additional experiments?

Where appropriate and if necessary (e.g., to verify your hypothesis), yes.

Your analysis should be supported by the relevant experiments, code and result tables.

The purpose of additional experiments is to support the question you are investigating, rather than simply to increase the number of experiments in the submission.

### Q9. How long should the discussion be?

As a guideline, we suggest approximately **500 words for each discussion**.

This is a guideline rather than a target that must be reached exactly. Focus on depth and clarity rather than word count.

### Q10. What does an excellent discussion look like?

An excellent discussion demonstrates:

* strong understanding of the task;
* critical analysis;
* clear logical reasoning;
* explanation of **why**, rather than merely **what** happened; and
* claims supported by experimental results and concrete examples.

A discussion that mainly reports scores without interpretation will normally receive less credit.
